Amber Food App: Reimagining the Order Flow

Solving choice paralysis through architectural minimalism.

Open Live Prototype

01. The Friction

Despite Amber’s legendary status in the Filipino culinary scene, its mobile application suffered from severe digital friction. Analysis of the native UI revealed critical pain points contributing to heavy drop-off rates

  • Severe Cognitive Overload: The legacy application relied on chaotic, multi-stepper quantity inputs on a single product screen, inducing massive decision fatigue.
  • Dated Information Architecture: Flat, dense text lists required endless scrolling to navigate through basic menu categories.
  • Fragmented Checkout UX: Raw, unparsed data strings created a high-risk drop-off point at the final conversion step.
  • Aesthetic Heavy-Handedness: Saturated yellow background blocks overwhelmed the interface, stealing focus away from the food.
Figure 1.0: Baseline analysis of the legacy ordering flow highlighting isolated product customization counters and unformatted physical address fields.
Figure 1.1: Cognitive friction points — flat, dense text-based menu categorization and isolated quantity controls requiring excessive scrolling.
Figure 1.2: Fragmented native data streams on the legacy payment modules leading to visual noise and unparsed data strings.

02. The Logic

To combat these pain points, I re-architected the information flow to isolate complex user decisions into bite-sized, componentized interactions.

  • Streamlined Decision Matrix: I condensed erratic quantity steppers into a single, clean "Variant Selection" modal with isolated radio buttons.
  • Componentized Checkout: I broke down chaotic text blocks into structured, isolated UI cards (Address, Order Summary, and Payment) for effortless scanning.
  • Dynamic Content Streams: I introduced visual circular category nodes at the top and a dedicated "For You" section to combat decision paralysis.
  • Whitespace Expansion: I aggressively utilized negative space to create a breathable, professional mobile experience.

03. The Polish

The visual layer was rebuilt around high-fashion minimalism, trading dense block colors for an editorial aesthetic where the food takes center stage.

  • Restrained Branding: I contained the brand’s signature yellow to a focused header card, letting a brutalist white canvas take over the interface.
  • Editorial Photography: Shifted focus to high-fidelity hero images of the dishes, making the product the primary visual anchor of the UI.
  • Clean Geometry: Pill-shaped quantity selectors, rounded cards, and floating input fields replaced sharp, default-looking borders.
Figure 2.0: Restructured user experience demonstrating heavy visual restraint, cinematic imagery, and component-based variant selection.
FFigure 2.1: Transformation of a fragmented path-to-purchase into a clean, normalized ecosystem with predictive UI modules.

04. The Result

By aligning the visual skin with modern systems architecture, the final product yields aggressive performance and usability metrics.

  • Frictionless Interaction: Reduced UI clutter by containing portioning logic within dedicated attribute sheets.
  • Maximized Trust: Eliminated confusing, unformatted checkout data in favor of a clean, highly secure, component-based layout.
  • Conversion Velocity: Projected faster path-to-purchase due to simplified navigation and predictive food recommendations.

From Rows to Insights: Redesigning DTS Contract Tracking

Transforming complex spreadsheet data into a high-impact, scannable visual dashboard.

Open Live Prototype

01. The Friction

Managing highly sensitive vendor and resource contracts is a high-stakes task. Previously, officers and staff relied on a massive, centralized Google Sheet to track contract statuses.

While functional for data entry, this setup created several massive user experience bottlenecks:

  • Cognitive Overload: Staff had to scroll through endless rows and columns to find specific contract statuses.
  • High Barrier to Insights: To see a simple breakdown of active vs. expired contracts, users had to manually create temporary pie charts or apply complex filters every single time.
  • Zero Visual Hierarchy: Critical, time-sensitive data (like contracts about to expire or requiring immediate offboarding) looked exactly the same as standard operations. There was no visual signaling to separate urgent tasks from the noise.
  • Filter Collision & View Disruption: Because multiple staff members accessed the same Google Sheet simultaneously, one user applying a filter to find specific data would instantly alter the view for everyone else. This caused constant confusion and interrupted real-time operations.
Figure 1.0: The original sheet caused major efficiency and collaboration bottlenecks. Manual data filtering, separate graph creation, and conflicting live filter views during shared access slowed down critical business decisions.

02. The Logic

The goal was to transform a dense spreadsheet into a scannable, multi-page visual application using Google Looker Studio. The logic behind the restructuring focused on progressive disclosure and independent user sessions.

  • Eliminating Filter Collision: By moving the team to Looker Studio, I ensured that when a user applies a dropdown filter (like filtering by a specific Point of Contact), it only changes the view on their specific screen. Multiple users can now analyze the same data simultaneously without disrupting each other's workflow.
  • Website-Style Navigation: Instead of cramming everything onto one overwhelming page, the dashboard was broken down into dedicated pages for targeted workflows.
  • The Power of Scorecards: At the very top of the main dashboard, I implemented high-impact scorecards. This served as a quick-glance health check for the entire contract ecosystem, completely removing the need to manually build charts just to see a summary.
  • Intentional Categorization: Scorecards and pages were logically grouped by the user's immediate needs: Active Contracts, Expired Contracts, Expiring Contracts, For Renewal Contracts, and For Offboarding Contracts.

03. The Polish

To make the dashboard look less like a default reporting tool and more like a bespoke enterprise application, I applied a minimalist aesthetic balanced with the company's brand identity:

  • Strategic Use of Brand Color: I utilized the company's vibrant Flush Orange (#FF8000) as a surgical accent. It was applied strictly to active navigation states, primary buttons, and the "Expiring Contracts" scorecard to draw immediate focus without causing visual fatigue.
  • Creating Visual Hierarchy: To solve the flat design of the spreadsheet, I used large, bold typography for numbers and soft, semantic background colors for data tables (e.g., soft greens for active, muted reds for offboarding). This instantly tells the user what needs attention.
  • Dynamic Interactive Filters: Added clean, accessible drop-down controls at the top of the pages so users could instantly slice data by Business Unit or Point of Contact (POC) without ever touching a spreadsheet filter.
Figure 2.0: A centralized landing view utilizing high-contrast, status-driven scorecards. Key metrics like Upcoming Expiry (in 20 days) are highlighted in active alert colors to prevent cognitive overload and guide immediate user prioritization
Figure 2.1: Deep-dive analytics for the expiring pipeline. By mapping numerical values directly to color-coded horizontal bars and double pie charts, users can instantly measure active risk across different corporate subsidiaries
Figure 2.3: Historical tracking of completed contracts. I introduced neutral tone palettes and soft-rose compliance tags to differentiate standard project completions from legal offboarding drivers without causing UI stress
Figure 2.4: A granular drill-down data table with multi-tier filter controls. This interface transitions users from macro-level insights to micro-level contract management, featuring localized 'Back to Overview' navigation for seamless backtracking.

04. The Result

The transition from a manual spreadsheet to a dedicated visual hub drastically shifted how the team interacts with contract data:

  • Instant Time-to-Insight: Staff went from spending several minutes filtering and calculating contract counts to seeing real-time tallies the exact second they open the page.
  • Zero-Effort Reporting: Presenting data to stakeholders no longer requires drafting manual charts. The dashboard itself now serves as the presentation layer for business decisions.
  • Proactive Operations: By surfacing "Expiring" and "For Offboarding" contracts front and center on the scorecard UI with high-contrast visual cues, the team can now spot upcoming deadlines at a glance, eliminating human oversight.
  • Interruption-Free Collaboaration: Team members can now work in parallel without fear of overriding or changing another user's active view, leading to a much smoother operational workflow.

Architecting Flow: The Timesheet Blueprint

Optimizing Enterprise Time Tracking: A High-Fidelity Case Study.

Open Live Prototype

01. The Friction

The current manual Excel process is a "Document-First" system that places the entire burden of data validation and payroll computation on human operators.

  • Redundant Manual Entry: Resources were forced to re-type static identity data (Name, ID, Manager) every month, leading to high "form fatigue" and preventable typos.
  • The Downstream Computation Burden: Because the Excel sheet is just a static grid that doesn't compute data, the heavy lifting of calculating total hours, night differentials, and split/half-days falls entirely on the HR and Finance teams.
  • The Contextual Blindness:: Because the Excel sheet is just a static grid that doesn't compute data, the heavy lifting of calculating total hours, night differentials, and split/half-days falls entirely on the HR and Finance teams.
  • The Validation Gap: Excel lacks "hard stops." This allows resources to input impossible entries (like logging 8 hours of work on a date already approved for a Full-Day Sick Leave) that HR has to spot manually.
  • Retroactive Recall Friction: Resources often fill out their logs at the end of the cutoff. Manually typing dates, times, and repetitive task descriptions into Excel cells creates high cognitive load and is prone to formatting typos.
From Friction to Flow: Mapping the transition from a disconnected manual process to a centralized digital ecosystem. This analysis highlights high-friction touchpoints—such as version control chaos and "email graveyard" feedback loops—justifying the need for a state-driven architecture and real-time audit trails.

02. The Logic

The solution was to move from a document-based workflow to a centralized, relational database. This allowed us to treat "Time" as data points rather than text in a cell.

  • Relational Architecture: By connecting a "User Table" to a "Timesheet Table," we ensured that identity data was pulled automatically, requiring zero input from the resource.
  • Automated Front-End Computation The system automatically calculates total hours, overtime eligibility, and split-day hours the moment the user enters their raw times, removing manual math from HR's plate.
  • Polymorphic Entry Logic: To handle the "Single Screen" constraint, selecting a "Day Type" (Work vs. Leave) dynamically toggles the required data fields so the user only sees what is relevant.
  • Chronological Spillover Handling: To support resources working overnight shifts, the system automatically detects when a "Time Out" is chronologically earlier than a "Time In" and applies a +1 Day logical offset without breaking the database query.
  • Cross-Field Validation: We designed a state-machine logic where a "Submit" action automatically updates the database and triggers a notification to the designated Line Manager.
Engineering a High-Fidelity Audit Trail: A state machine designed to eliminate the "Excel Black Box." This architecture governs data visibility and permissions across the lifecycle, ensuring that only verified, locked records proceed to payroll integration.

03. The Polish

The UI was intentionally kept minimalist and "Feature-First." The goal wasn't visual flair, but cognitive ease—ensuring the user could get in, log their time, and get out.

  • Contextual Headers: A profile-driven header that confirms the user’s current project and manager without any manual input.
  • The Smart Switcher: A prominent toggle at the top of the entry screen that morphs the form based on the selected day's context (Work vs. Leave)
  • Automated Split Detection: When a resource logs their raw time and toggles a "Half-Day Leave," the UI dynamically splits the day and displays the payable hours automatically.
  • Spillover Visual Indicators: For night shifts, a subtle UI pill reading +1 Day appears next to the time-out field to confirm the system understands the overnight shift.
  • Batch Generation Form: A form allowing users to select multiple dates at once to generate identical schedules and tasks, heavily reducing manual typing.
Figure 1.0: Launch screen for 'Worklog Manager,' introducing the core brand identity with a clean, high-contrast visual design that emphasizes accessibility and clarity.
Figure 2.0 The primary 'Overview' dashboard, utilizing distinct, color-coded components to provide immediate visual feedback on weekly hours and project distribution.
Figure 3.0: Core 'Time Log' interface, designed with clear segmentation for day types and integrated selectors, ensuring efficient data entry. This screen shows the standard workflow for logging individual days.
Figure 4.0: Introduction of the 'Bulk-Generate' feature, a workflow optimization that allows users to populate and review multiple logs in a single batch process based on a defined date range.
Figure 5.0: Architected a multi-state lifecycle that transitions local drafts into a formalized monthly audit. Features state-aware indicators (Approved, Pending, Rejected) and integrated reviewer feedback loops to streamline asynchronous corrections and ensure payroll data integrity.
Figure 6.0: The complete 'History' management system. This view demonstrates how submissions are tracked and organized, utilizing the prototyped date range component to filter and manage historical data.
Figure 7.0: Focused 'User Profile' view, consolidating account-level settings, primary work shift definitions, and managerial contact information in an easily accessible single screen.

04. The Result

By shifting to a service-oriented architecture, we didn't just build a tool; we reclaimed time and accuracy for the entire department.

  • Elimination of Identity Typos: REmoving manual entry for Name and ID completely eliminated payroll delays caused by bad identity data..
  • Massive Reduction in HR Processing Time: Shifting computation to the point of entry removed the need for manual calculations by the finance team.
  • 100% Data Integrity: Cross-field validation eliminated "logical errors" (overlapping leaves/shifts) before they reached payroll.
  • Drastically Lowered User Friction: Moving from cell-typing to smart defaults and bulk generation minimized the time it takes for a resource to complete their monthly submission.
